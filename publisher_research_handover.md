## About Tau (what the publishers will be judging)

**One line:** Go is war and chess is a battle. Tau is single combat: two pieces on one board, and the only way to win is to push your opponent off.

**The game**
- Two-player abstract strategy. Each player has one tripod piece on a round board.
- On your turn you pin one foot and swing the other two in an arc. Movement is continuous, not square to square.
- You can cross only one line per turn. You win by pushing an opponent's foot off the rim.
- No luck, no hidden information, no draws (every turn must actually move).
- Inspired by sumo and judo: balance, angles, distance and leverage rather than capture.
- Designed around 2018, digital version made recently.

**Physical prototype**
- A 267 mm brushed steel disc (430 stainless) in a printed base, with nylon tripod pieces that have small magnets in the feet. A 400 mm "heirloom" size is planned.
- Sourcing is done: steel discs from about US$4–5 each, pieces about US$3–4 each at small volumes.
- Photos exist (yellow board with blue and red tripods). Ask Liam for the best one; don't pull images from the web.

**Digital version (free):** https://tau-game.com
- Plays in the browser, with online ranked play, 11 AI levels, local play, replays, and 10 languages.
- About 35 ranked players so far.
- Liam trained his own AlphaZero-style neural net on it. A depth report (tau-game.com/depth) found no forced win and play that stays varied as strength goes up.
- A Steam version is nearly ready.

**Traction so far (confirm current numbers with Liam before finalising)**
- Posts on r/abstractgames, r/boardgames, r/BoardgameDesign and others, with good discussion. One r/abstractgames post had 332 views in 2 hours.
- Threads on BoardGameGeek, a French forum (CNRL, 258 views) and a Korean one (BoardLife, 237 views).
- A YouTube creator played it in an indie games video.
- A waitlist on tau-game.com for a physical set.

**Audience:** Go players first, then people who like mathematical or generative art, combinatorial game theory, martial arts (especially grappling), and minimalist design.

**Things to keep in mind about fit**
- Components cost more than cardboard (steel, magnets, nylon). Publishers who already make premium or unusual-component abstracts are the best fit.
- Liam wants to **keep the digital rights** (tau-game.com and Steam), so note any publisher whose terms make that hard.
- The name "Tau" may need a subtitle for trademark reasons (Games Workshop owns "T'au Empire"). Working title: **Tau: Abstract Grappling**. Use it on the sell sheet and flag it as provisional.
