## Part 1: What Tau is

Tau is a two-player abstract strategy game designed by Liam in Melbourne. It began in 2018 as a handmade tabletop game inspired by sumo and judo.

**How it plays.** Each player has one piece, a tripod standing on three feet, on a round steel board. On your turn you pin one foot and swing the other two around it in an arc. The board has printed lines (two rings and two facing arcs), and a piece may cross only one line per turn. You win when one of your opponent's feet goes off the edge of the board. There's no luck and no hidden information. The pitch line is: "Go is war and chess is a battle. Tau is single combat."

**Where it is now:**
- A free web version at tau-game.com, with ranked online play, an AI ladder of 14 levels, replays and 11 languages.
- A Steam version (Tau: Abstract Grappling) with its store page in progress.
- A neural network Liam trained on the game, which plays the top AI levels.
- A physical prototype that exists and works, but nothing has been made in quantity yet.

**What these parts are for.** Liam is about to launch a challenge: the first person to beat AI level 14 playing as Red wins a physical set, and anyone who beats level 13 gets a free Steam key. He's also sending sets as gifts to martial artists who might feature the game, starting with Firas Zahabi (Tristar Gym, Montreal) and later Demetrious "Mighty Mouse" Johnson. Those sets need to exist first. This order is **10 sets made as cheaply as possible**, which Liam assembles and paints himself in Melbourne. The budget for everything, including posting two sets overseas, is **A$500**.

## Part 2: The physical set, part by part

Why each spec matters, so you can judge listings.

### The board: a steel disc

- **Size:** a 267 mm circle (266.7 mm exactly), 1.5 to 2 mm thick, brushed finish.
- **It must be magnetic steel. This is the most important spec in the whole order.** The piece feet carry small magnets that grip the board. That grip is part of how the game feels and how its rules work.
  - **430 stainless steel** is magnetic and doesn't rust, so it's the right grade.
  - **304 and 316 stainless, the most common grades, are NOT magnetic.** A disc in either grade is useless for Tau.
  - Aluminium, acrylic or wood won't work either.
  - Many listings just say "stainless steel". Only accept one that says 430, ferritic or magnetic.
- **Thickness:** the quote Liam has is for 1.5 mm. His 3D model of the base was drawn for a 2.0 mm disc. Note which thicknesses each listing offers so Liam can decide.
- **Edges** should be smooth or deburred, because bare cut steel can be sharp.

### The board art: a printed ring pattern

- The lines go on the disc, either as a thin vinyl decal or printed straight onto the steel (UV printing).
- **A decal has to be thin,** about 0.1 to 0.2 mm, so the magnets still grip through it.
- There's a known risk that a sticker bubbles or peels where feet slide over it. Gloss or satin vinyl looks closer to the game on screen than matte does.
- The artwork files are already made: `physical/board_art/tau_board_267_slate_RGB.pdf` (dark grey) and `..._dojo_RGB.pdf` (warm sand). Liam uploads them himself.

### The pieces: two tripods, one red and one blue

- **Size:** about 86 × 75 × 54 mm, with feet about 46 mm from the centre.
- **Material:** Liam prints them himself on his Ender 3 in PETG or PLA+. Resin or silk PLA would snap at the ankles.
- **Paint:** red and blue spray paint over a plastic primer.

### The magnets: tiny discs in the feet

- **The size isn't settled yet.** Liam's 3D model has pockets for **5 mm × 2 mm** discs, while his notes mention 6 mm × 2 mm and 2 mm × 2 mm. He has to measure a prototype foot to know which.
- Open listings for **5 × 2 mm** first, and also note prices for **6 × 2 mm**.
- **Grade:** N48 or N52 neodymium. Polarity doesn't matter, because they only touch steel.
- **Quantity:** at least 100. They're cheap at that number.

### The base: the ring the disc sits in

- A plastic rim that holds the disc flush with its top edge, so a piece slides right off the edge rather than hitting a lip. It's printed in 4 snap-together segments.
- Liam prints it himself in PETG, so it comes out of the same filament roll. No separate purchase is needed, apart from an optional quote for having it printed by a service.

### Packaging and paperwork

- **Box and insert:** for these 10 sets, Liam makes simple boxes and inserts from heavy card using his own box design. Each box is a roughly 300 mm square, so the card needs to be large and heavy.
- **Rules card:** a business-card-sized card with a QR code that links to the rules and tutorial on tau-game.com.
- **Outer mailer:** a sturdy postal box for the two sets going overseas. A boxed set weighs about 1.5 kg.
