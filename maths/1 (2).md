# Tau: mathematically sound n-ply proof structure

## Claim level and autonomous direction

This work establishes a conditional mathematical proof architecture for any finite ply bound n, and an executable proof kernel for exact finite test models. It does not establish a Tau win at any new depth. The missing engine-exact geometric verifier is deliberately not replaced by samples, ideal physics, or unchecked certificate assertions.

The autonomous direction is to prove engine-exact bounded reachability. Ideal contact regimes remain useful for discovering compact transition enclosures, but cannot discharge an engine obligation without a proved bridge.

## 1. Define the game before quantifying over moves

Let S be complete game states at committed turn boundaries. Let t(s) be the player to move, L(s) the legal completed-turn controls, and T(s,u) the resulting state. Fix a protagonist P. Let G_P be terminal states won by P according to the pinned game semantics.

A state must retain all data that can affect a future transition. In the shipped source this includes both poses, active player, ko history, committed ply count, terminal winner and adjudication state. Any additional persistent flags must be retained or proved irrelevant. Mid-turn transition checking also needs pivot, planted foot, signed accumulated angle, direction lock, crossing bookkeeping, contact state, snapshots, and limit flags. A six-coordinate pose key is not an n-ply state key.

Source observations:
- `endTurn()` checks the active piece first, then the opponent, otherwise hands over the turn.
- `commitTurn()` performs ko history handling and increments the committed ply counter, with cap adjudication.
- `applyPlanSearch()` calls `endTurn()` without the real ko/history/cap updates.
- `applyPlan()` legalizes the proposed plan and uses its own input schedule.
- `applySwing(dA)` partitions each call into ceil(abs(dA)/stepMax) equal internal steps.

Consequently, neither a search wrapper nor a fixed stop angle alone defines the full shipped turn relation. Do not call an opponent-off observation during a swing a terminal win without certifying the actual stopping/commit outcome and precedence.

### Semantics tags

Every certificate must state one of these, without mixing them:
1. Shipped input semantics: legal complete input traces, including call partition and permitted pre-lock direction changes.
2. Fixed-controller semantics: a named, source-pinned controller's completed turns.
3. Ideal continuous semantics: the separately specified unilateral-contact system.

For the broad engine theorem, retain complete legal input traces. A fixed-controller theorem is a narrower milestone, not a proof against every human move. Infinite or noncommitting input traces require an explicit convention; a theorem counting completed plies does not assert that a player must finish a turn in bounded wall-clock time.

## 2. Exact finite-horizon recurrence

Define W_0^P = G_P. For k >= 0, define W_(k+1)^P as G_P together with nonterminal states satisfying:

- If t(s)=P: there exists u in L(s) with T(s,u) in W_k^P.
- If t(s)!=P: L(s) is nonempty and for every u in L(s), T(s,u) is in W_k^P.

Terminal losses and draws are never inserted by the predecessor rule. Empty legal sets are not vacuous wins: they need a source-derived no-move terminal rule, or remain unknown.

This means a win within at most k completed plies, not exactly k, not an evaluation score, and not a guarantee that a sampled best response is optimal.

For an enclosure relation E(s,u) containing all possible actual successors, use the stronger condition E(s,u) subset W_k^P, with E(s,u) nonempty for legal controls. This is sound for deterministic transitions enclosed by intervals and for explicitly adversarial nondeterminism. It can be incomplete because enclosure artefacts may introduce spurious losing states.

### Proposition 1: bounded-strategy equivalence

For a fully specified deterministic, perfect-information, turn-based game with the stated terminal/no-move conventions, s is in W_k^P if and only if P can force a terminal P-win within k completed plies.

Proof by induction on k. At k=0, the claim is exactly the definition of G_P. At a P-turn, a witnessing legal move enters W_(k-1)^P, where the induction hypothesis supplies the remaining strategy. Conversely, the first move of a bounded winning strategy must have that property. At an opponent turn, every legal first move must admit a remaining bounded winning strategy; conversely, after observing that move P follows its corresponding strategy. Terminal losses and draws cannot satisfy the claim. Nonempty legal sets prevent vacuous success. This completes the induction.

The two-ply dead-position target is the opponent-turn instance: for every legal reply, there exists a legal P-response whose committed outcome is a P-win. The response may depend on the observed reply; it must not depend on future opponent choices.

## 3. Finite region certificates, without discretizing the game

A node carries a state region R, a nonnegative integer budget k, a semantics/source pin, and one of four proof rules.

### Terminal

Prove R subset G_P using the actual terminal rule. A numerical foot margin alone is insufficient if the commit rule, active-piece precedence, or cap adjudication can change the outcome.

### State split

Prove R subset union_i R_i. Each child proves R_i subset W_k^P. This consumes no ply. The proof graph must be finite and acyclic, so same-budget splits cannot create circular arguments. Children may overlap; all boundary states must be covered.

### Existential strategy node

For every s in R, prove a specified strategy u=pi(s) is legal and its full successor enclosure lies in child winning regions of budget at most k-1. A constant witness uniformly legal on R is sufficient but stronger than necessary. State splitting supports different witnesses on different regions. A state-dependent witness needs a checked function and domain, not just a list of sampled good moves.

### Universal reply node

Cover the joint legal state-action domain

    D_R = {(s,u): s in R and u in L(s)}

by finitely many checked domains D_i. For each D_i, prove every actual successor is enclosed by child regions of budget at most k-1. The cover is of state-action pairs, not just angles at the center state. Move limits, ko exclusions, call schedules and terminal branches can depend on s.

Unknown legality can be conservatively included at universal nodes if a sound transition enclosure is supplied. It cannot be used as a legal existential witness. Omitted branches require a checked proof of illegality.

### Proposition 2: certificate soundness

Suppose a finite acyclic certificate has valid source/semantics pins, sound terminal checks, sound state and legal-domain covers, legal existential strategies, sound successor enclosures, and a budget decrease of at least one on each actual move edge. Then every state in its root region lies in W_k^P for the root budget k.

Proof: use reverse topological induction on the certificate DAG. Terminal nodes establish the base assertion. State splits inherit it from a covering union. At an existential node, the checked legal strategy and successor inclusions give the existential predecessor condition. At a universal node, every legal state-action pair belongs to a checked domain and every actual successor lies in a child winning region, giving the universal predecessor condition. Smaller child budgets imply the required bound. No cycle can supply its own conclusion. Therefore the root assertion follows.

This theorem is conditional on the local obligations being proved. The executable prototype currently discharges them only for exact finite test models. It refuses a Tau backend rather than trusting the current geometric checker.

## 4. Why this does not imply a finite cover always exists

Finite piece geometry does not prove finitely many contact switches. A finite ply bound does not bound input calls within a turn. Open legality boundaries, zero throw margins, grazing, singular active sets, and arbitrarily long input partitions can prevent a finite robust cover of the desired form.

A compactness argument is available only after proving a compact legal domain and local open neighborhoods with robust valid witnesses. Ko and strict boundary rules can violate those hypotheses. Without them, the architecture remains sound but may fail to certify a true win. Unknown is not losing.

## 5. The geometric backend's exact obligations

A turn-transition proof must certify:

1. Legal start state and control domain, including minimum move, lock, pivot, crossings, ko and permitted stopping.
2. Every input-call partition and accepted internal substep relevant to the chosen semantics.
3. Self-off rollback and crossing rollback before collision resolution.
4. Snapshot contact geometry, pair scan order, capped corrections, horizontal-fraction floor, deep-crossing branch, hub pushes and iteration limits.
5. A sound enclosure of every intermediate state used by subsequent branch decisions.
6. Committed terminal outcome or next-turn state, including ko history reset/append, ply count and cap adjudication.
7. Source-specific floating-point semantics, including a justified transcendental enclosure or a precisely pinned runtime model.

Ideal active-set regimes may provide a candidate enclosure, but a verified discrepancy bound or exact sequential propagation must connect them to these operations. A source hash prevents drift; it does not prove arithmetic correctness.

The previously found hubV tangential defect means the old pin cannot be admitted as a trusted leaf. The local repaired vertex map and scalar ideal flow are useful lemmas, not universal mixed-contact or whole-turn theorems.

## 6. Executable kernel and evidence

Files under `nn/research/nply/`:
- `kernel.js`: independent exact finite-model verifier and separate certificate generator.
- `test.js`: bounded minimax cross-check and adversarial certificate mutations.
- `example-certificate.json`: a two-ply universal-reply/existential-response fixture.
- `test-results.json`: reproducible test counts.
- `schedule-probe.js` and `schedule-results.json`: actual shipped call-partition comparison.
- `source-manifest.json`: SHA-256 source pins and explicit unresolved obligations.

The finite-model kernel checks turn ownership, uniform witness legality, all legal replies, successor inclusion, state split coverage, terminal winner, source/model hash, depth budget, and cycles. It rejects unsupported Tau semantics and nonterminal states with unspecified empty-move rules. Tests are synthetic unit tests, not invented Tau trajectories.

The generator and verifier were checked against independent bottom-up bounded minimax on 200 seeded finite games, including cycles, both players and depths 0 through 6. Results: 25,200 comparisons, 6,145 accepted generated proofs, and 13 rejected malformed/unsupported cases. These tests validate implementation behavior, not a machine-formalized proof of the JavaScript kernel.

## Writing and claim discipline

The structure separates the induction theorem, executable finite-model tests, actual source observations, and unresolved Tau transition obligations. This prevents a correct n-ply quantifier structure from disguising an unsound geometric leaf.


## 9. Exact rational continuum-cover lemma

`rational-cover.js` verifies a rational target rectangle against a finite union of rational rectangles, retaining open and closed endpoint flags. It uses BigInt rational comparisons, not floating-point tolerances.

Lemma: collect all target and cover endpoints on each axis. Partition the target into endpoint singleton atoms and open intervals between consecutive endpoints. Each input interval has constant membership on every atom. Hence each cover rectangle has constant membership on every product atom. The target is covered if and only if every product atom is contained in at least one cover rectangle. The implementation checks this finite condition exactly. A resource-limit refusal is unknown, never covered.

Ten regression cases check ordinary coverage, a missing singleton, singleton repair, a gap smaller than double endpoint resolution, open/closed targets, a two-dimensional corner hole, a full two-dimensional cover, a point target and an empty cover. These are synthetic exact-arithmetic tests. They establish no Tau collision bound and do not reduce arbitrary input traces to rectangles.

## 10. Shipped schedule probe and scope

The actual `applySwing` probe compares a 24-degree attack supplied as one call, twenty-four 1-degree calls, or forty-eight half-degree calls. From the same constructed rigid initial reply, the first and last schedules differ by 0.007457893644474233 position units and 0.09227952933775466 degrees of victim rotation. Both produce victim-off in this example. This falsifies angle-only trajectory identity, not a throw claim.

The intended broad theorem concerns normal core committed-turn rules. Coaching, lab setup overrides, online/network clocks, surrender and UI mode-specific outcomes are excluded unless separately modeled. Human move legality still requires the normal input-handler guards, not merely the ability to call `commitTurn()` directly. The source manifest records this scope and the unresolved backend obligations.
