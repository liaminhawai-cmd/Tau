# Where the trained net and L11 disagree

Generated by `disagree.js` riding along on `frank-tournament.js`: at every 3rd ply, both the
trained net (iteration-45 `best.json`, `[94,96,64,48,1]`) and L11's hand-tuned `ladderEval` rank the
**same** candidate pool (4°-sampled sweeps), and the 50 positions where their top picks diverged most
were kept. Divergence is measured symmetrically in z-units of each evaluator's own score spread, so
sharp and calm positions are comparable.

## 1. The net plays big sweeps; L11 plays small ones

| | mean | median | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| nn sweep | 0.559 rad (32°) | 0.524 rad (30°) | 0.105 – 1.047 |
| L11 sweep | 0.355 rad (20°) | 0.314 rad (18°) | 0.052 – 1.047 |

**The net picks the larger sweep in 38/50 (76%).** They agree on the pivot foot in only 14/50 and on
the direction in 21/50 — at these positions they are not making the same move slightly differently,
they are making genuinely different moves.

## 2. What the net gives up to do it

Each of L11's eval terms, scored in isolation, for the net's pick minus L11's pick (L11 eval points,
so already weighted — `zone` carries 12, `park` 8, `oppFree` -1.1, `triMe` 0.2, `margin` 1):

| term | mean Δ | median Δ | net's pick better in |
|---|---|---|---|
| `margin` | -4.65 | -3.49 | 13/50 |
| `zone` | **-8.64** | **-12.00** | **3/50** |
| `park` | **-8.00** | **-8.00** | **1/50** |
| `oppFree` | -2.73 | -1.72 | 10/50 |
| `triMe` | -1.32 | -0.64 | **21/50** |

Two things stand out.

**`triMe` is near parity (21/50).** This is the judo feature — on record as the strongest single
predictor found, and one of the terms `features.js` block D hands the net directly rather than making
it rediscover. The near-coin-flip says that worked: the net has genuinely internalised it and simply
has its own opinion about when it applies.

**`park` is 1/50, and the median Δ is exactly -8.00** — precisely one park unit at L11's weight. The
net's preferred move parks exactly one fewer foot, almost every single time. `zone` (3/50) is nearly
as one-sided. These are L11's two heaviest terms.

## 3. The generator, not just the evaluator

`nnai.js` has **no park handling at all** — no `PARK_HI`/`PARK_LO`, no park-stop recording. It walks
each sweep in blind 3° steps and evaluates whatever waypoints happen to land.

That is the exact omission `index.html:4210-4214` documents as having silently broken the p3 ladder
rungs:

> since the turn-start grace went magnet-scale (build 130), the coarse sampleDeg stops almost never
> land a foot genuinely ON a line, which silently took the park/double-cross play away from the p3
> rungs (**measured: L8 fell from ~63% to ~43% vs L7**, whose dedicated recorder kept its aim)

L8-L11 were fixed by explicitly recording up to two exact in-band park stops per sweep. The net never
got that fix. A ~20 percentage-point swing was measured when the hand-tuned rungs lost park play —
roughly 140 Elo, or more than a full rung.

So parking is under-served on the net side twice over: many parking moves are not in its candidate
list at all, and when they are, its evaluator does not rate them.

## 4. Caveats

- **50 positions**, all drawn from hybrid-brain and L10 games — not a neutral sample of Tau positions.
- The shared 4° pool under-samples exact park positions for *both* sides, so section 2 measures
  "which of the parks both could see does each prefer", not the full picture.
- **This does not prove the net is wrong.** It is possible `zone`/`park` are overweighted in L11 and
  the net is right to discount them. The head-to-head results are the arbiter, and the early
  tournament signal (L10 beat C2 4-0, L11 beat C1 4-0) leans toward a real net blind spot — but that
  is equally consistent with L10/L11 simply sitting 2-3 rungs above the net's current L8-9 strength.

## 5. What follows from it

The cheapest test, independent of the hybrid work: **port the p3 park-stop recorder into
`nnai.js`'s candidate generation** and A/B the same net with and without it. It changes only what the
net is allowed to consider, not what it has learned, and there is a measured precedent that the same
omission cost a hand-tuned rung ~20 points.
